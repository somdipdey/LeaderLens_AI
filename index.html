Privacy-preserving leadership self-reflection

LeaderLens AI

A browser-based tool for understanding dark trait signals, strengthening ethical leadership capacities, and creating a practical AI-assisted development plan.

Privacy-first design No account. No server. No tracking. Results stay in your browser unless you choose to export or share them.

Purpose of this tool

LeaderLens AI supports private, voluntary self-reflection for people who want to become more self-aware and effective leaders. It helps you explore behavioural tendencies that may create interpersonal risk, while also identifying prosocial leadership capacities that can be strengthened through deliberate practice.

The tool produces two score profiles: one for Dark Tetrad reflective signals and one for light / ethical leadership capacities. You can then copy a structured prompt into a GenAI assistant such as ChatGPT, Copilot, Gemini, or Claude to generate a safe, non-diagnostic 2–4 week development plan.

Important: This is not a clinical, diagnostic, recruitment, performance-management, or disciplinary tool. Scores are reflective indicators only. Use them to guide self-learning, leadership development, and behaviour improvement; do not use them to label yourself or others.

What are Dark Tetrad tendencies?

The Dark Tetrad refers to four socially aversive, non-clinical personality tendencies that can appear in everyday interpersonal behaviour:

  • Narcissism: status-seeking, entitlement, admiration needs, image protection, or difficulty receiving criticism.
  • Machiavellianism: strategic manipulation, cynical calculation, selective disclosure, or using influence mainly for personal advantage.
  • Psychopathy: lower emotional sensitivity, impulsivity, risk-taking, reduced remorse, or rule-bending in pursuit of outcomes.
  • Sadism: enjoyment, satisfaction, or indifference around another person’s discomfort, humiliation, or social defeat.

In this tool, these are treated as reflective signals to manage, not identity labels or diagnoses.

What are light / ethical leadership capacities?

Light and ethical leadership capacities are prosocial behaviours that can reduce interpersonal harm and strengthen trust:

  • Empathy: perspective-taking, emotional awareness, and attention to how decisions affect others.
  • Humility: accurate self-awareness, teachability, willingness to admit mistakes, and appreciation of others’ contributions.
  • Ethical leadership: fairness, integrity, transparency, confidentiality, and willingness to challenge harmful conduct respectfully.

The aim is to use self-awareness to build practical leadership habits, not to create a fixed personality profile.

How the scores are calculated

Each trait or capacity is assessed using seven original framework-informed items. Each item is answered on a 1–5 agreement scale. For each trait, the seven item scores are averaged and converted to a 0–100 score using: ((mean - 1) / 4) × 100, rounded to the nearest whole number.

Equal weighting: within each trait, every item contributes one seventh of the trait score (approximately 14.29%). Reverse-scored items are first converted using 6 - response, so that higher final values consistently reflect a stronger reflective signal for dark traits or a stronger reported capacity for light/ethical leadership traits.

This equal-weighted scoring is intentionally transparent and avoids hidden algorithmic weighting. It is appropriate for a self-reflection and improvement tool where the items are original and framework-informed, because users and researchers can clearly see how each response contributes to the final profile.

Interpretation guide

  • Dark traits: higher scores are reflective signals, not labels. Focus on behavioural triggers and safeguards.
  • Light traits: higher scores indicate self-reported strengths. Lower areas can guide development priorities.
  • Mixed profiles are normal: people can show both risk tendencies and ethical strengths depending on context.

Question-to-trait mapping and rationale

This section makes the scoring logic transparent. To minimise response bias, complete the Self‑Assessment before reading the mapping. The items are original prompts informed by established constructs; they are not copied from the cited questionnaires or scales.

Transparency note: The mapping explains how each item contributes to a reflective trait signal or leadership capacity. Some items are reverse-scored because agreement indicates a protective behaviour rather than a risk tendency.

Why these 49 items?

LeaderLens AI uses 49 items: seven items for each of seven dimensions (narcissism, Machiavellianism, psychopathy, sadism, empathy, humility, and ethical leadership). This structure was selected to balance construct coverage, participant burden, and practical usefulness. A much longer questionnaire could capture more nuance, but it may reduce completion quality and discourage honest reflection; a shorter tool would be easier to complete but could underrepresent important workplace behaviours.

The item pool was not cherry-picked from copyrighted scales. Instead, each item was written as an original workplace-relevant behavioural prompt mapped to established constructs from the Dark Triad, SD3, SD4/Dark Tetrad, workplace mistreatment, Light vs Dark Triad, ethical leadership, empathy, and expressed humility literatures. Each dimension includes a mix of direct behavioural indicators and reverse-scored protective behaviours so the tool can capture both risk signals and safeguards.

For scoring transparency, all seven items within a dimension have equal weight: each contributes approximately 14.29% of that dimension’s score. Equal weighting is used because LeaderLens AI is a transparent self-reflection tool rather than a psychometrically reweighted clinical, recruitment, selection, or performance-management instrument.

Scoring method and weighting

Responses use a 1–5 agreement scale. Reverse-scored items are converted using 6 - response. For each trait, the seven adjusted item scores are averaged, then converted to a 0–100 score using ((mean - 1) / 4) × 100. This means a trait score reflects the participant’s average response pattern for that construct, not a diagnosis or fixed personality label.

The 0–100 format is used because it is easier for users to interpret than raw scale means. The score bands are intentionally cautious: dark-trait scores are described as lower, moderate, or elevated reflective signals; light/ethical leadership scores are described as development priority, emerging capacity, or reported strength.
